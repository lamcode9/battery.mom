Site Architecture Audit

battery.mom · 2026-06-23 · the move from a consistent skin to a coherent spine

The design-system migration made every page look like it belongs to the same site. It did not make every page belong to the same argument. This document fixes the second problem: it states the one thesis, sorts all 29 routes into four topic pillars and five page archetypes, resolves the duplications, and defines the navigation and page template that turn "consistent-looking chunks" into one publication.

1 · The diagnosis: skin vs. spine

The first analysis found a pile of individually-built pages — boxes nested in boxes, inconsistent colour, no shared language, no narrative. Phase 1 fixed the surface: shared ink/paper/brand palette, serif headings, zero nested borders, verified across every route.

What was optimised was the wrong layer for the goal. "Zero nested borders, same colours everywhere" is visual consistency — a skin. It removed the ugliness. It did not create cohesion — a spine: one controlling idea, a clear hierarchy, recognisable page types, and links that turn pages into a journey. The skin shipped; the spine is this document.

2 · The thesis

battery.mom tracks how fast batteries, solar, and EVs are deploying across Southeast Asia — and turns that into decisions you can act on.

Every page does one of two jobs. It either helps you TRACK the transition (what is happening in the world) or DECIDE your move (what it means for you). The macro story and the scoreboards are Track. The comparison tool and the calculators are Decide. The home page already opens on this idea — the rest of the site should visibly serve it.

3 · The root cause of "disparate chunks"

The site was organised by content format, not by topic. Someone who cares about EVs had to know that EV material was scattered across three separate menus: EV (compare), Scoreboard → EV Adoption, and Calculators → EV vs ICE / Charging cost. Five concrete symptoms:

  1. No stated thesis on the surface. The one sentence above was implied, never declared as the thing every page serves.
  2. The nav was a flat pile of eight. Home · Story · EV · BESS · Scoreboard · Calculators · Insights · About. "Story" — meant to be the spine — was just tab #2 with no priority.
  3. Two front doors to the same story. /state-of-battery-power (narrative) and /scoreboard/energy (dashboard) tell the same global-battery story in two formats, competing instead of laddering overview → drill-down.
  4. Tools scattered by format. Calculators lived under both /calculators/* and /bess/*; EV had a tool (/ev) and a board (/scoreboard/ev) in different menus.
  5. Every page a one-off shape. No two pages shared an anatomy, so there was no recognisable "this is what a battery.mom page looks like."

4 · The four pillars

Reorganise by subject, not content-type. Four topic pillars, each laddering from its headline view down to its tools. No route folders move — this is purely how the site is navigated and cross-linked.

6 · Overlaps & decisions

Story ↔ Energy Scoreboard (the big one). Same dataset, two formats, two front doors. Decision: the Story is the narrative entry; the scoreboards are the drill-down. The nav now places both under Big Picture with the Story first, and the Story should end by handing off to the live board. Not a merge — a ladder.

EV tool ↔ EV Adoption board. Different intents (compare a car vs. see a country's uptake), kept — but now both sit under the EVs pillar so the relationship is visible.

BESS Adoption board currently reads as a data-gap audit rather than a dashboard. to reframe — either commit to it as a dashboard or rename it to what it is.

7 · The page template (archetypes)

Every page should be recognisably one of five types, each with a fixed anatomy. This is what kills the one-off-shape problem.

ArchetypeAnatomyExamples
Pillar landingHero thesis → what's inside → cards to children → next pillarHome, /bess, /scoreboard, /calculators, /insights
Tool / calculatorHero → plain-language result sentence → inputs → results → assumptions → related tools/ev, /bess/*, /calculators/*
Data scoreboardHero → headline number → charts → method/sources → the Story/scoreboard/*
Article / editorialHero → standfirst → body → sources → related reading + tool/state-of-battery-power, /bess/case-studies, /insights/[slug]
Utility / metaHero → content → back to relevant pillar/about, /contributors, /contact, /api-docs, /embed-widgets

The shared rule across all five: every page ends by pointing to the logical next page. That connective tissue is what makes moving through the site feel like a journey rather than teleporting between unrelated sites.
